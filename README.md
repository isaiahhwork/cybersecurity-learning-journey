## Network Topologies
**Star Topology** 
Each device connects to a central device. (Typically a switch or router.) It is common in homes and offices, it is easy to add/remove devices and identify problems. If the central router or switch is compromised, breaks or fails all devices will lose connection
**Bus Topology** 
All devices are connected to one, main cable. It is the most cost effective and simple topology, often utilised for simple networks. If the main cable happens to break or fail, all packets are stopped. Additionally, on a bus topology if too many packets are sent out at once the system can potentially fail and is slow with many devices connected at once.
**Ring Topology** 
Data literally travels in a ring, there is fewer collisions of data. However, one broken connection, once broken cable will disrupt the whole network, and it is harder to expand and change. 
**Mesh Topology** 
Devices connect to several other devices. It is extremely reliable because there are multiple paths, and has backups. This means that if a cable were to fail, or break it will not stop the network. However, it is extremely expensive to implement and fix as there are many connection to be made.
**Tree Topology** 
Devices are arranged in levels, alike branches in a tree. It is optimal for large networks, easy to organise devices. However, if a main branch or central device fails, it could affect most of the network.

**Overall** 
Learning network topologies helps me understand how devices communicate, how each topology can fail and the security risks. The thing I took from this topic the most is that it is vital to protect the central router or switch, as it is very important to the network.
